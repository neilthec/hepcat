The reference checkout is HEPCAT publication commit `aa676a9c198f9b50a682adc2bc98be590b4adb0e`. To regenerate the table, run `python3 export-publication-catalog.py /path/to/publication catalog.tsv --kernel /path/to/WolframKernel`. The 38 independent catalog entries come from `tests/benchmarks/reproduction/100k/catalog/input-expressions.wxf` inside the committed `tests/benchmarks/benchmarks.zip`. Its SHA-256 is `be9bdbce817965e227352301a49e8b96ce501b8cf6240673fce0a70d86ba2339`. Two more entries exchange photon legs 3 and 4 in the catalog's four-point mixed-helicity expressions. The last two evaluate the physical and spurious five-positive-photon formulas from `tests/QED-6-point-construction.nb` (SHA-256 `557691155ce5c706a18aab079942541c92a629233a656fc64078fe9d444b090e`). Each expression retains its sign and model parameters. The scope of each real model parameter is named `PUBLICATION` for use with a separately loaded QED model.

Each row gives the publication Wolfram package's `SpinorExpressionComplexity` and Mathematica's `LeafCount` for the complete expression. The former weights momentum insertions, products and denominator structure. The latter measures the syntax tree, including the representation of model parameters. A different factorization of the same numerator can change the two measures in different directions.

The publication's own `drafts/QED-paper/amplitude-review.md` and `amplitude-validation.json` (under `EarlierFormulaReview.FinalMixedExpressions`) record earlier, independently worked improvements. For the three-photon mixed families those targets are 62 physical and 63 spurious (349 leaves in either form). For four photons with one opposite helicity they are 327 physical (1,232 leaves for +++-, 1,220 for +---) and 416 spurious (1,458 leaves in both cases). For the balanced four-photon case the working spurious form agrees with the 712 and 2,311 leaves in the catalog. These are separate complexity targets. The table preserves exactly the signed formulas from the checked-in publication validation campaign.

### Rerun the Mathematica comparison

From the HEPCAT root, the following command compares one ordinary public construction with its best recorded weighted score. It also requires the physical channel, spurious pole, high-energy, reference and signed Feynman checks applicable to the case. The external particle names and the bound are the only catalog information supplied to this test.

```sh
HEPCAT_PUBLICATION_CASES='spurious/eeAAAA/++--' python3 tests/checks/run-wolfram-tests.py \
  --suite publication-qed-construction-tests.wl --timeout 1800
```

Use a comma-separated list for selected cases. For the full catalog, set `HEPCAT_PUBLICATION_CASES=all` and allow time for all 42 calls, for example `--timeout 10800`. The runner timeout applies to the entire suite. Each call reports its elapsed time, HEPCAT complexity, Mathematica leaf count, Feynman sign and check totals. The external rate comparisons and the all-positive formula study use separate drivers. [External validation](external-validation.md) and [formula validation](all-positive.md).

The [Mathematica results](wolfram-comparison.tsv) give the measured score for each call to `ConstructAmplitude`. The [C++ results](complexities.tsv) give the corresponding scores for the native constructor. Both use HEPCAT's weighted `SpinorExpressionComplexity`. The elapsed times in the Mathematica table are individual runs on a shared machine. On the same host and seed, the physical three-photon `++-` construction went from 84.7 seconds and score 71 to 13.7 seconds and score 62. Other jobs were running during both measurements.

Across the 42 cases both constructors reached the same weighted scores. Eleven scores are lower than the publication catalog and 31 are equal. The Mathematica constructions passed 619 final checks across their 42 printed reports. With five positive-helicity photons the spurious form now assembles a factored current for each subset and preserves the factorization when orienting spinor chains. Its weighted score falls from 1160 to 920 and its Mathematica leaf count from 9887 to 7946. Ten Mathematica results have more leaves than the catalog. The largest increase occurs in the six-fermion result, where the weighted score falls from 4608 to 3168 and the leaf count rises from 12632 to 16705.

For the four-photon `AllSpurious` family, the optimized SPINAS code generated by Mathematica passed all 1,600,000 labeled comparisons and all 100,000 polarization sums on the publication's saved MadGraph momenta and weights. A fresh MadGraph calculation at the 25 momenta with previously flagged helicities confirmed the replay. The [Mathematica export report](wolfram-external.json) records the input expressions, compiler precision, residuals and data provenance.

For five positive-helicity photons, we exported the 920-score expression returned by Mathematica. Its squared amplitude agreed with a fresh MadGraph calculation at all 100,000 momenta. The largest relative difference was `2.13e-11` for the explicit massive-spin sum and the generated `amp2()`. The [seven-point report](wolfram-seven-madgraph.json) identifies the expression and normalization.

For a signed comparison of the native expressions, we also evaluated the publication's independent Feynman diagrams on 100,000 momenta per family and pole form, first with aligned spin bases and then with rotated bases. We compared the largest component difference with the largest Feynman component in each tensor. All tensors passed the relative tolerance of `1e-7`. The largest relative difference was `1.29e-8`. In the aligned basis some individual components are close enough to zero that their own relative error exceeds this tolerance. The [signed results](signed-feynman.json) retain those counts as well as the tensor errors, seeds, and generated-source hashes.
